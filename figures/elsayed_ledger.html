An Arab- and Muslim-American funding base

Name-token classification of the named dollars — the roster's most distinctive feature, and a story either way it's told: a historic national mobilization of Arab- and Muslim-American donors behind a candidate who would be the first Muslim U.S. senator. Names matching Arab, Persian, Urdu or Turkish token lists (including Levantine Christian surnames) account for 69.5% of named dollars but only 29.3% of gifts — an average gift of $782 vs. $329 overall — against a population base of roughly 2%: about 35× overrepresented by dollars. The community writes the checks; the small-dollar base is broader.

Read the bounds, not just the headline. These are name tokens, not a religion or race census — shared names (Ali, Khan, Shah, Hanna) inflate the cut, converts and Anglicized names deflate it, and the strict cut requiring first and last name to match is 26.4%. The figure has grown with each recall pass (49% → 64.7% → 69.5%): the latest pass reclassified the formerly 20.8%-ambiguous bucket down to 11.9% using ~450 additional surname tokens curated on both sides (Bazzi, Itani, Iqbal … and Griswold, Costello, Katz …), applied only to previously unclassified rows. What remains ambiguous is 12,214 mostly small gifts averaging ~$100 — Western given names with uncommon surnames. The population reference is Arab Americans (≈1.2%) plus U.S. Muslims (≈1.1–1.3%), overlapping, ≈2% combined. Top-20 donor lists run ~85% Arab-or-Muslim-named because the top is concentrated — but the top 100 donors are only ~9% of all named dollars, so the share is base-wide, not a top-donor artifact.

Method & caveats

Source: six Class-A FEC electronic filings for committee C00902668 (2005714, 2005716, 2005717, 2005719, 2005720, 2005721), Schedule A individual contributions, April 2025 through July 15, 2026. The 31,180-row named roster sums to $10,265,431.61, matching Form 3 line 11a(i) exactly.

A "donor" is a unique last name + first name + state; shared names merge and movers split, so donor counts are estimates. Occupation and employer are self-reported by donors. The $4.14M unitemized total has no names, occupations, or locations by law, so every per-donor figure here describes only the itemized 71% of receipts. Refund/redesignation entries filed later can revise individual totals.

FEC contributor data is a public record; federal law prohibits its use for soliciting contributions or for commercial purposes. Super-PAC and other outside money is not part of this donor base.
